Claim-to-evidence logic

A valid test is not automatically the right proof.

CNP asks a more precise question: does this method, on this product, in this population and at these time points, justify the exact wording communicated to consumers?

Example claim“Hydrates the skin”
Potential evidence

Instrumental hydration measurements on the finished product, supported by an appropriate protocol.

CNP examines

Product tested, time points, baseline, sample, statistics and whether the wording matches the measured effect.

Example claim“Supports the skin barrier”
Potential evidence

TEWL or another relevant barrier endpoint, often interpreted together with hydration and use conditions.

CNP examines

Whether the method measures barrier function rather than merely consumer-perceived moisturisation.

Example claim“Suitable for sensitive skin”
Potential evidence

Tolerance or in-use testing in a population that reflects the intended sensitive-skin claim.

CNP examines

Population definition, inclusion criteria, supervision, reactions and the limits of the conclusion.

Example claim“Improves the appearance of wrinkles”
Potential evidence

Imaging, profilometry or another validated instrumental endpoint on the finished product.

CNP examines

Endpoint selection, time points, comparator, statistical analysis and risk of overstating the effect.

Important boundary

CNP adds an evidence standard. It does not replace legal responsibility.

The company and the Responsible Person remain accountable for cosmetic compliance. CNP independently reviews the documentary and scientific coherence of the product and its claims.
